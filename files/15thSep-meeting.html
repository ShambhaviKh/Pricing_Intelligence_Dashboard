💰 What Constitutes Price?

Price is not a single number - it's built from multiple components. Understanding these helps us know what to optimize and what affects pricing decisions.

Six Price Components

📍
Base Price
List price at product level. Foundation for all decisions. Varies by product family.
📦
Volume Adjustments
Bulk discounts or premiums. High-volume orders get better rates. Different by segment.
👥
Segment Adjustments
Personal vs Enterprise vs Partner. Different commercial agreements. Reflects customer type.
⏰
Temporal Adjustments
Promo pricing, end-of-quarter push, seasonal, inventory clearance urgency.
🔗
Product Mix Effects
Bundle pricing, cross-product discounts. Watch cannibalization between product families.
🏆
Customer Tier
Premium vs standard customers. Relationship/contract-based pricing. Long-term accounts.

How Price Flows Through the Data

🔢 The Basic Formula
Revenue per Transaction = (Base Price + Component Adjustments) × Units Ordered
This means to optimize price, we need to understand how each component affects:
→ Total Revenue generated
→ Profit Margin realized
→ Units actually sold
→ Customer satisfaction/returns

Price Calculation Examples

Example 1: Enterprise Segment High Volume
Base Price: $1,000 | Volume Discount: -10% | Segment Adjustment (Enterprise): -5% | Final Price: $855/unit
Result: Lower margin per unit, but higher volume typically compensates
Example 2: Personal Segment Single Unit
Base Price: $1,000 | Volume Adjustment: none | Segment Adjustment (Personal): +5% | Final Price: $1,050/unit
Result: Higher margin, but lower volume from premium pricing
Example 3: End-of-Quarter Clearance
Base Price: $1,000 | Temporal Adjustment (Clearance): -20% | Final Price: $800/unit
Result: Move inventory, accept lower margin to clear stock

Key Pricing Questions to Answer in Meeting

❓ Confirm with Team:

  • ✓ Which price components currently exist in business?
  • ✓ Are all 6 components used, or only some?
  • ✓ Who sets each component? (Finance, Sales, Product, etc.)
  • ✓ Are there rules/constraints on adjustments?
  • ✓ How often are prices updated?
  • ✓ What's the relationship between Revenue & Price in our data?

💡 Why This Matters for Pricing Intelligence

  • We're not inventing pricing - we're optimizing what already exists
  • Our model needs to understand current price structure to make recommendations
  • Different components respond differently to optimization
  • Segment-specific strategies require separate optimization rules

🎯 What Should We Optimize For?

The dashboard must support multiple optimization scenarios, not just one target. Different business situations require different objectives.

Five Core Optimization Scenarios

Scenario 1: Maximize Revenue
Goal: Capture maximum total sales value
When to use: Market expansion, high-volume growth priority
Price approach: Lower prices to drive volume
Risk: Margin compression, reduced profitability
KPI: Total Revenue
Scenario 2: Maximize Margin
Goal: Capture highest profit per unit
When to use: Profitability priority, lower volume acceptable
Price approach: Higher prices for better margins
Risk: Lower volume, lose market share
KPI: Total Margin or Margin %
Scenario 3: Maximize Units
Goal: Sell maximum number of units
When to use: Market penetration, building customer base
Price approach: Aggressive pricing to maximize volume
Risk: Extreme margin loss, commoditization
KPI: Total Units Sold
Scenario 4: Inventory Clearance
Goal: Clear excess or end-of-life inventory
When to use: Product discontinuation, warehouse capacity
Price approach: Aggressive discounting to move stock
Risk: Trains market to expect low prices
KPI: Inventory Reduction Target
Scenario 5: Balanced (Weighted)
Goal: Balance multiple business priorities
When to use: Multiple competing objectives matter
Price approach: Weighted formula: w1×Rev + w2×Margin + w3×Units
Advantage: Flexible, context-specific, testable
KPI: Custom weighted score

Scenario Comparison: Pros & Cons

🔴 Maximize Revenue

Simple objective, easy to measure
Good for growth phases
May destroy margins
Volume doesn't always follow

🟢 Maximize Margin

Improves profitability
Focuses on quality revenue
May leave volume on table
Risky if competition underprices

🔵 Maximize Units

Build market share quickly
Create switching costs
Severe margin impact
Unsustainable long-term

🟡 Inventory Clearance

Solves immediate problem
Frees up capital
Very low margins short-term
Market perception risk

⚪ Balanced/Weighted

Reflects real-world complexity
Context-specific, adjustable
More complex to implement
Requires defining weights

Recommended Approach: Flexible Optimization

✅ Build the dashboard to support ALL scenarios

  • ❶ Create pricing models for each scenario
  • ❷ Allow users to select objective OR input custom weights
  • ❸ Show trade-offs (e.g., "Max margin reduces volume by X%")
  • ❹ Enable what-if analysis for testing scenarios
  • ❺ Track results vs actual performance

Critical Questions to Answer in Meeting

❓ Key Decisions Needed:

  • Which scenario is PRIMARY for your business?
  • Are there secondary objectives that matter?
  • Does this change by segment? (e.g., Enterprise = margin, Personal = volume)
  • Does this change by product? (e.g., New product = volume, mature = margin)
  • Are there hard constraints? (e.g., "never go below 20% margin")
  • How often do objectives change?
💡 Dashboard Design Implication
Don't hardcode one objective into the model. Build a Decision Matrix where users can:
→ Select the scenario (or create custom weights)
→ See recommended prices for that objective
→ Understand trade-offs vs other scenarios
→ Compare to actual results over time

📈 Key Trends & Correlations

Understanding how metrics relate to each other helps us set realistic optimization targets and understand business dynamics.

Core Metric Relationships

📊 Revenue vs Units: Volume-Driven or Price-Driven?
What EDA shows: Analyze if revenue growth comes from more units sold or higher prices.
Decision Impact: If revenue is volume-driven, pricing down won't hurt. If price-driven, volume sensitivity matters.
Questions to answer: How does 10% price change affect units? What's the elasticity by product?
💰 Revenue vs Margin: High Revenue ≠ High Profit
The Gap: Some products drive high revenue but lower margins (discounted to boost volume).
Decision Impact: Can't optimize just revenue. Must consider cost structure & margins together.
Action: Create Revenue vs Margin scatter plot. Identify which quadrant each product is in.
📉 Margin % by Product: Natural Variation
Typical finding: Different products have different natural margin %. Not all should target same margin.
Why?: Cost structure varies (premium vs budget products), market competition differs, customer segments vary.
Action: Benchmark margins by product family. Understand which are structurally high/low margin.
⏰ Temporal Patterns: Seasonality & Trends
What to look for: Are there seasonal peaks? End-of-quarter surges? Holiday effects?
Decision Impact: Pricing can differ by time. Clearance urgency varies by quarter.
Metrics to track: Revenue trend, margin trend, units trend - all by month and fiscal quarter.
👥 Segment Differences: Personal vs Enterprise vs Partner
Expected finding: Each segment likely has different volume/margin profile.
Decision Impact: May require different pricing strategies per segment. Enterprise might want margin, Personal wants volume.
Action: Compare Revenue, Margin, Margin %, Units across segments. Note differences.
📦 Product Mix: Returns & Cancellations Matter
Key insight: Reported revenue ≠ actual realized revenue if returns/cancellations high.
Decision Impact: High return rates reduce real profitability. May indicate pricing/product mismatch.
Metrics: Return rate by product, cancellation rate by segment - watch for outliers.

Expected Analysis Outputs

Visualizations to Create:
Revenue trend by fiscal quarter (14 quarters showing growth/decline)
Margin trend by fiscal quarter (compare to revenue trend)
Units sold trend (shows volume changes independent of revenue/margin)
Correlation matrix (Revenue, Units, Cost, Margin - see relationships)
Revenue vs Margin scatter (identify product quadrants)
Performance by segment (compare Personal, Enterprise, Partner)
Top products by revenue (show concentration)
Return rate by product (quality/satisfaction indicator)
Monthly seasonality (peaks and valleys)
